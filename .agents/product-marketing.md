## Product Overview
**One-liner:** Two friends, two phones, one temple: fire and water must save each other.
**What it does:** An online 2-player co-op puzzle platformer for phones. Hot Boy (fire) and Cool Girl (water) get through a dark jungle temple together. Each is safe in their own element and in danger in the other's, so every level needs both players helping each other.
**Product category:** Mobile co-op puzzle platformer (Android and iPhone, landscape).
**Product type:** Game. Today a free web game (GitHub Pages, opens from a link, no download); next a Capacitor app on Google Play, later the App Store, maybe Steam.
**Business model (decided 2026-10-31):** No ads, ever. Chapters 1–2 and the level designer are free; one $2.99 purchase (Google Play, in the Android app) unlocks every other chapter forever. **Friend's pass:** if either player in a room owns the game, the room plays everything, so only one of two friends pays. The website is the free demo that leads to the app. Not launched yet.

**What's in the game now (2026-11-06):**
- 7 chapters, 35 levels, each proven winnable. Chapters 8–9 ("Don't touch": if the two touch, both burst into steam; and a finale) are planned and approved but not built, which would make 9 chapters and 45 levels.
- Buttons, levers, lifts, gates and trapdoors.
- **Different eyes:** some things are visible to only one player, so you must describe them out loud.
- **Password gates:** animal carvings hidden across a chapter. Each team gets its own random code, entered on symbol stones by taking turns.
- **Both mixed (Chapter 7):** each player sees only half of the secret code, so the two must tell each other their half.
- A level designer: build a level, finish it together to get a ✅, share it by link.
- A duet soundtrack where each character's music layer gets louder while they move.
- Each chapter is its own area of the temple with its own background and tune; Chapters 1–6 open with a short riddle on a stone tablet.

## Target Audience
**Who plays:** Pairs of friends, couples, siblings, cousins who want something to play *together* on the phones they already have, often on a voice call or sitting together.
**Primary use case:** "We're both on our phones and bored: let's play something together right now."
**Jobs to be done:**
- Have fun *with* a specific person, not with strangers
- Start in seconds from a shared link: no accounts, no downloads (web), no ads
- Feel clever together: solve a puzzle as a team
**Age:** not decided yet (proposed: 13 and up on Google Play, to stay out of the Families rules). The fire-and-water genre is popular with children and teens, so keep art and copy "stylish, not cute" and never target children in ads.
**Use cases:**
- Long-distance friends or couples on a call
- Two people on the same sofa, each with their own phone
- Parent and child, older and younger sibling (the controls are only left, right, jump)

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** Good co-op games mostly live on consoles and PC, and both people usually need to own the game. On phones, "multiplayer" usually means competing with strangers, not cooperating with a friend.
**Why alternatives fall short:**
- The classic fire/water browser games are played on one keyboard: they need two people at one computer.
- Big co-op hits need consoles or PCs and a full-price purchase.
- Most mobile games are full of ads and timers.
**Emotional tension:** "I want to *do* something with my friend, not just text them."

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** The classic fire-and-water browser games (the genre's inspiration). They share one keyboard, so you can't play from two places, and the art is dated. We are an original game: never say we are "Fireboy & Watergirl" or a sequel (hard limit L4).
**Secondary:** Console/PC co-op adventures (the It Takes Two style). Great, but they need a console or PC and usually two copies or a pass.
**Indirect:** Co-op party games on PC, and competitive mobile multiplayer. Fun, but strangers or competition rather than two friends helping each other.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- Made for phones: one player per phone, online, landscape, thumb controls
- Starts from a link in under 30 seconds: no accounts (pillar P5)
- Only one friend needs to pay (friend's pass, proposed)
- Puzzles where talking is the puzzle: "different eyes" (each sees different things) and team codes (each team's password is different)
- No ads, no timers, no energy
- A level designer where a level only counts as verified once two friends have beaten it together
**Why that's better:** Lower cost and less setup than console co-op; more *together* than other mobile games.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "My friend won't pay too." | They don't have to: with the friend's pass, one owner opens every chapter for both. |
| "Is it just the old fire/water game?" | Original characters, art, music and new mechanics (different eyes, password gates, level designer), on two phones online. |
| "Does it need good internet?" | It connects phone to phone; a two-phone test over mobile data worked well (2026-10-31). Don't promise "any network". |
| "I don't have anyone to play with." | Then it's not for you: it's built for two people who know each other. |

**Anti-persona:** Solo players looking for a single-player game, and people who want to play with strangers.

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** Bored of solo phone games and ad-filled freebies; can't play console co-op apart.
**Pull:** Free to try, from a link, and you play *with* your friend in seconds.
**Habit:** Texting, scrolling, playing solo games side by side.
**Anxiety:** "Will it be too hard for my friend?" "Will we need to pay twice?" "Will it lag?"

## Brand Voice
| Attribute | Means | Doesn't mean | Example line |
|-----------|-------|--------------|--------------|
| Playful duo | Opposites teasing each other: he rushes in, she thinks first | Childish or cutesy | "He jumped. She sighed. They both made it." |
| Short and clear | One idea per line; anyone gets it instantly | Jargon | "Two phones. One temple. Save each other." |
| Warm about friendship | Playing *together* is the point | Mushy | "Better with your person." |

**Tone by context:**
- Launch: excited, simple, "free to try"
- Social: funny co-op fails and "aha" moments; the 5-second clip
- Errors / support: calm, friendly, plain language
**Never use (and why):** Other games' names or characters (L4); fake urgency; claims without proof.

## Goals
**Business goal:** First prove that strangers love the free chapter, then launch on Google Play with the one-time unlock.
**Conversion actions, in order:** finish Chapter 1 → invite a friend → come back another day → buy the unlock (`docs/MARKETING.md` section 4).
**Measurement (no server, no analytics, on purpose):** Play Console statistics, UTM-tagged store links per source, platform stats for clips, Discord counts, and a tester form. See `docs/MARKETING_PLAN.md` 2.1.
**Current metrics:** None written down yet. Playtests happened (2026-10-31: ~20 people played the levels, 2 the whole game), but the three numbers and quotes are still to be recorded. Don't invent them.
